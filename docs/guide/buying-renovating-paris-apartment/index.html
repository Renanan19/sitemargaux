Buyer’s guide: buying and renovating a Paris apartment from abroad

What happens, in what order, and what it costs beyond the price. Written for buyers in the United States and Canada. Last checked November 15, 2026.

Who is buying

Americans and Canadians can buy property in France. There is no nationality restriction on residential purchases, and you do not need to live in France. Owning an apartment does not give you a right to stay beyond the usual 90 days in any 180.

Americans were the leading nationality among foreign non-resident buyers in Paris in 2025: 220 purchases, 26% of that group. The median purchase by an American non-resident buyer in Paris was €864,000 for 65 m² (700 sq ft). Foreign non-residents made 3.7% of all Paris purchases (Notaires du Grand Paris, June 25, 2026).

The notaires’ June 2026 press release gives no separate figure for Canadian buyers.

The purchase, step by step

  1. Offer. Made in writing through the seller’s agent. Once it is accepted, the notaires prepare the contract.
  2. Preliminary contract. You sign and pay a deposit, commonly around 10% of the price, held by the notaire. You then have 10 days to withdraw without giving a reason. The period starts the day after the signed contract is notified to you (first presentation of the registered letter).
  3. The wait. 2 to 3 months while the notaire searches the records and any mortgage is approved. This is when plans are drawn and contractor quotes are collected.
  4. Final deed. Signed at the notaire’s office, in person or by power of attorney. You get the keys that day.

Costs on top of the price

Budget roughly 7% to 8% of the price for what are called notaire fees on an existing apartment. Most of it is transfer tax. Your notaire gives the exact figure before you sign.

If you use a buyer’s agent, the published fees of Paris agencies run from about 2.5% to 3% of the price plus VAT.

Mortgages

French banks do lend to non-residents, but fewer of them lend to US taxpayers, and terms vary widely. Speak to a broker who handles non-resident borrowers before you make an offer that depends on a loan.

Renovating in a copropriété

Nearly every Paris apartment is in a copropriété (the building’s co-ownership). The building’s rules and the syndic (the building’s managing agent) shape what you can do. Copropriété and syndic explained.

General meetings are often held once a year. If your project needs a vote, the date of the next meeting can set your timetable, so it is worth asking before you buy.

What a renovation costs

It depends on the condition of the wiring, plumbing and structure far more than on the floor area. Figures quoted per square meter online range so widely that they mislead. I give an estimate for the apartment in front of me, at the visit, and a full set of contractor quotes once the plans are drawn. Published cost ranges per m².

Renting it out

  • Tourist rentals of a second home need a change-of-use authorization with compensation; without it the fine is up to €100,000 (City of Paris).
  • A primary residence may be rented to tourists up to 90 days a year, with a registration number (City of Paris).
  • A bail mobilité is a furnished lease of 1 to 10 months for qualifying tenants (Service-Public).
  • Since January 1, 2025, G-rated homes can no longer be rented on a new lease (Ministry of Ecology). F follows in 2028 and E in 2034 (Service-Public).

Tax questions for your adviser

I am an interior architect, not a tax adviser. These are the questions to take to someone who handles both French and US or Canadian returns.

  • French income tax on rent received by a non-resident, and how your home country credits it.
  • French social charges on rental income for residents of the US or Canada.
  • The real-estate wealth tax, IFI, which applies above €1,300,000 of net taxable property (Service-Public). Non-residents are assessed on French property only (Service-Public).
  • Whether to buy in your own name or through a company, and what that changes on resale and inheritance.
  • French inheritance rules, which differ from those in North America.

Short answers

Can Americans buy property in France?

Yes, and so can Canadians. France places no nationality restriction on buying residential property. Ownership does not give a right to live in France.

How long does a purchase take?

Expect 2 to 3 months between the preliminary contract and the final deed. The buyer has a 10-day withdrawal period. It starts the day after the signed preliminary contract is notified to the buyer (first presentation of the registered letter).

Do I need the building’s approval to renovate?

Not for work inside your apartment that touches nothing shared. Work affecting load-bearing walls, floors, shared pipes or the façade needs a vote of the owners at the general meeting of the copropriété.

Is there a wealth tax on French property?

The French real-estate wealth tax (IFI) applies when net taxable property exceeds €1,300,000. Non-residents are assessed on their French property only.

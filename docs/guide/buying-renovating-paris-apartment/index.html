Buying and renovating a Paris apartment from abroad

What happens, in what order, and what it costs beyond the price. Written for buyers in the United States and Canada. Last checked 11 November 2026.

Who is buying

Americans and Canadians can buy property in France. There is no nationality restriction on residential purchases, and you do not need to live in France. Owning an apartment does not give you a right to stay beyond the usual 90 days in any 180.

Americans were the first nationality among foreign non-resident buyers in Paris in 2025: 220 purchases, 26% of that group. The median purchase by a foreign non-resident in Paris was €864,000 for 65 m². Foreign non-residents made 3.7% of all Paris purchases (Notaires du Grand Paris, 25 June 2026).

The notaires publish no separate figure for Canadian buyers.

The purchase, step by step

  1. Offer. Made in writing through the seller's agent. Once accepted, the notaires prepare the contract.
  2. Preliminary contract. You sign and pay a deposit, commonly around 10% of the price, held by the notaire. You then have ten days to withdraw without giving a reason.
  3. The wait. Two to three months while the notaire runs searches and any mortgage is approved. This is when plans are drawn and contractors priced.
  4. Final deed. Signed at the notaire's office, in person or by power of attorney. You get the keys that day.

Costs on top of the price

Budget roughly 7% to 8% of the price for what are called notaire fees on an existing apartment. Most of it is transfer tax. Your notaire gives the exact figure before you sign.

If you use a buyer's agent, the published fees of Paris agencies run from about 2.5% to 3% of the price plus VAT.

Mortgages

French banks do lend to non-residents, but fewer of them lend to US taxpayers, and terms vary widely. Speak to a broker who handles non-resident files before you make an offer that depends on a loan.

Renovating in a shared building

Nearly every Paris apartment is in a co-owned building, a copropriété. The building's rules and its manager, the syndic, shape what you can do. Copropriété and syndic explained.

General meetings are often held once a year. If your project needs a vote, the date of the next meeting can set your timetable, so it is worth asking before you buy.

What a renovation costs

It depends on the state of the wiring, plumbing and structure far more than on the floor area. Figures quoted per square metre online range so widely that they mislead. Renting it out

  • Tourist lets of a second home need a change-of-use authorisation with compensation; without it the fine is up to €100,000 (City of Paris).
  • A main home may be let to tourists up to 90 days a year, with a registration number (City of Paris).
  • A bail mobilité is a furnished lease of 1 to 10 months for qualifying tenants (Service-Public).
  • Apartments rated G cannot be newly let since 1 January 2025; F from 2028; E from 2034 (Ministry of Ecology).

Tax questions for your adviser

Margaux is an interior architect, not a tax adviser. These are the questions to take to someone who handles both French and US or Canadian returns.

  • French income tax on rent received by a non-resident, and how your home country credits it.
  • French social charges on rental income for residents of the US or Canada.
  • The real-estate wealth tax, IFI, which applies above €1,300,000 of net taxable property; non-residents are assessed on French property only (Service-Public).
  • Whether to buy in your own name or through a company, and what that changes on resale and inheritance.
  • French inheritance rules, which differ from those in North America.

Short answers

Can Americans buy property in France?

Yes, and so can Canadians. France places no nationality restriction on buying residential property. Ownership does not give a right to live in France.

How long does a purchase take?

Expect two to three months between the preliminary contract and the final deed. The buyer has a ten-day withdrawal period after signing the preliminary contract.

Do I need the building's approval to renovate?

Not for work inside your apartment that touches nothing shared. Work affecting load-bearing walls, floors, shared pipes or the façade needs a vote of the co-owners.

Is there a wealth tax on French property?

The French real-estate wealth tax (IFI) applies when net taxable property exceeds €1,300,000. Non-residents are assessed on their French property only.

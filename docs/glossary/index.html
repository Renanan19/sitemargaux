Paris property glossary

The French words you will meet when buying and renovating an apartment in Paris, each defined in a sentence or two.

Stouls Studio, Paris. Updated 11 November 2026.

Explained in full

  • Pied-à-terre

    A small second home in a city, kept for regular short stays and not used as a main residence.

  • Haussmann apartment

    An apartment in one of the stone buildings put up during and after the rebuilding of Paris led by Georges-Eugène Haussmann from 1853 to 1870.

  • Chambre de bonne

    A former servant's room under the roof of a Paris apartment building, usually 6 to 12 m², reached by a service staircase.

  • Copropriété and syndic

    The French form of shared building ownership: each owner holds a private unit and a share of the common parts. The syndic is the manager the owners appoint.

Buying and renting terms

  • Notaire

    A public legal officer who drafts and registers property sales in France. Every purchase goes through one. Buyer and seller may each have their own at no extra cost, since they share the fee.

  • Compromis de vente

    The preliminary sale contract. It fixes the price and conditions, and the buyer pays a deposit, commonly about 10%. The buyer then has ten days to withdraw.

  • Acte de vente

    The final deed, signed at the notaire's office two to three months after the compromis. Ownership and keys change hands that day.

  • Frais de notaire

    The costs paid on top of the price at signing, about 7% to 8% for an existing apartment. Most of it is transfer tax, not the notaire's own fee.

  • DPE

    Diagnostic de performance énergétique, the energy rating from A to G that every property for sale must show. G-rated homes cannot be newly let since 1 January 2025.

  • Loi Carrez

    The law requiring the seller of a unit in a shared building to state its private floor area. Only floor with at least 1.80 m of ceiling height counts.

  • Bail mobilité

    A furnished lease of 1 to 10 months, not renewable, with no deposit, for tenants such as students or people on a work assignment.

  • Chasseur immobilier

    A buyer's agent who searches on the buyer's behalf and is paid by the buyer, typically a percentage of the price.

  • Dépôt de garantie

    The security deposit a tenant pays at the start of a lease.

  • Meublé

    Furnished. A furnished let must include a set list of furniture and equipment defined by decree.

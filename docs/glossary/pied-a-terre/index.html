What is a pied-à-terre?

A pied-à-terre is a small second home in a city, kept for regular short stays and not used as a main residence. The French words mean “foot on the ground”.

Stouls Studio, Paris. Updated November 11, 2026.

Where does the word come from?

It is French, literally “foot on the ground”: a place to set your foot down when you arrive. English borrowed it in the nineteenth century and kept the spelling, hyphens and accent.

It is pronounced roughly “pyay-dah-TAIR”. The plural is pieds-à-terre.

What counts as a pied-à-terre?

  • It is in a city, and usually an apartment.
  • It is small: a studio, one bedroom, sometimes two.
  • It is not where the owner lives most of the year.
  • The owner uses it regularly, a few days or weeks at a time.

A house in the country where you spend August is a vacation home. An apartment near the office that you sleep in three nights a week is a pied-à-terre. So is a one-bedroom in the 6th arrondissement owned by a couple from Boston who come four times a year.

Short answers

What does pied-à-terre mean?

A pied-à-terre is a small second home in a city, kept for regular short stays and not used as a main residence. The French words mean "foot on the ground".

How do you pronounce pied-à-terre?

Roughly "pyay-dah-TAIR". The d of pied links to the à, and the final e of terre is silent.

What is the plural of pied-à-terre?

Pieds-à-terre. Only the first word takes an s, and the pronunciation does not change.

Is a pied-à-terre the same as a vacation home?

Not quite. A vacation home is usually somewhere you go to rest. A pied-à-terre is in a city you return to often, for work, family or pleasure, and it is typically an apartment.

Can a foreigner own a pied-à-terre in Paris?

Yes. France has no nationality restriction on buying residential property. Owning one does not give a right to live in France: US and Canadian citizens may stay 90 days in any 180 without a visa.

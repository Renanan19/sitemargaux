Stouls Studio / Apartment sourcing

Buying an apartment in Paris, with someone who will have to renovate it

The listings of apartments for sale in Paris look alike from across the Atlantic. Margaux searches, visits and reports. You see every apartment on video with her notes on what it needs, and you make an offer knowing what the works will cost.

What you get for each apartment

  • A walk-through video, room by room, with the street and the stairwell.
  • Measurements and a sketch of the layout as it is and as it could be.
  • Her notes on structure, damp, wiring, plumbing, windows and heating.
  • The energy rating and what it means for renting the place.
  • The building: recent works, works voted but not yet paid for, monthly charges.
  • A first renovation estimate, so the comparison between two apartments is the full price of each.

Photos in a Paris listing are taken with a wide lens on a sunny day. The visit is where you learn that the bedroom faces a wall two metres away, or that the only place for a washing machine is the entry hall.

What she looks for

The apartments worth buying from abroad are usually the ones that show badly. A dark corridor that can be opened up. A kitchen at the back that can move to the living room. A 50 m² one-bedroom that becomes a two-bedroom once the layout is redrawn.

Those are the ones other buyers pass over, and the ones where design adds the most value for the money spent.

She also tells you which ones to leave. A beautiful flat under a roof that the building has not voted to repair is not a bargain.

Questions about sourcing

How is this different from a property finder?

A property finder judges location and price. Margaux also judges the works: structure, plumbing runs, electrical state, what the building will allow. You get a purchase price and a renovation estimate side by side.

Do you find off-market apartments?

Sometimes, through agents and contractors she works with. Most good apartments in Paris are listed, and go quickly. What matters is seeing them in the first days and knowing at once whether the layout can be fixed.

Who signs the purchase?

You do, with a notaire, in your own name or through your own company. The sale itself goes through licensed Paris estate agents and the notaire. Margaux's part is the brief, the visits and the assessment of each apartment. She does not hold your money and is not a party to the sale.

Tell Margaux what you want in Paris.

Budget, neighbourhood if you have one, and whether you plan to live in it or rent it. She replies in English.
